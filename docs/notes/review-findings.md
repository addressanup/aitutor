

# REVIEWER: feasibility

## overall
The plan's control and demonstration architecture is genuinely sound — I independently verified the CGEventTap synthetic-vs-hardware discrimination, the screenSaver-level overlay technique, the Figma localhost-WebSocket bridge, and the cache mechanics, and the possession protocol is the best-engineered part of the document. But the observation/assessment side ships a fatal unverified assumption: the single most likely point of technical death is the FCP observation layer — the plan prices 'honest assessment' on a silent background FCPXML poll that macOS does not permit (export is user-initiated-only; the workaround is control-stealing GUI automation the plan's own possession rules forbid) plus an AXObserver notification stream whose density on FCP's custom timeline canvas nobody has measured, with the only fallback being the vision cadence the economics section itself disqualifies at $26/hour. If FCP practice can't be watched semantically, P3/P5 degrade to sparse screenshots and debrief-time grading, and the product's core differentiator quietly becomes a video course with a voice. As amended — checkpoint-based artifact exports, a week-1 AX-density spike with a named fallback, the corrected Watch-gate arithmetic, and an honest headcount/schedule — I would stake money on the engineering; I would not stake it on the current draft, which would sail through M0 planning and hit these walls in weeks 2 and 12 respectively.


## [BLOCKER] feasibility-1: Assessment ground truth rests on a background FCPXML poll that macOS does not allow — and that the plan's own possession protocol forbids

**Argument:** Section 3.3 and design-observation-assessment.md specify 'adapter state polls: FCPXML export every ~3 minutes and at checkpoints' as the continuous, ~$0 artifact channel, and §1 claims 'the assessment ran on the exported FCPXML, not on vibes.' But there is no programmatic way to trigger an FCPXML export. Apple's own FCPX Workflows Guide confirms: 'The user initiates an export with the share command in Final Cut Pro X' — the ProVideo Asset Management Apple Events suite is a response protocol that lets an external app configure an export the USER started, never trigger one (verified against developer.apple.com/library/archive/.../FinalCutProXWorkflowsGuide/Exporting). FCP's AppleScript dictionary is standard-suite-only (the plan's own research memo says so). The only path is GUI automation: drive File > Export XML plus a save sheet via AX/keystrokes — exactly how CommandPost's 'Hidden FCPXML Import/Export' features worked. That means every 'poll' during observed practice (a) requires the tutor to act while possession is LEARNER_DRIVING, contradicting §3.2's 'the tutor may request control; it can never take it'; (b) steals keyboard focus from the practicing learner mid-attempt; and (c) is aborted by the plan's own ≤100 ms hardware-interrupt rule the moment the learner moves the mouse. The artifact-first assessment order (artifact ±2.0 logits vs fluency ±0.7), the rubric checkpoints ('artifact assertion runs first'), and the honest-praise wiring all assume this channel is free and silent. It is neither. Nothing in the risk register or M0 gates covers this.

**Suggested change:** Redesign §3.3's artifact channel: (1) replace 'FCPXML export every ~3 minutes' with explicit checkpoint exports at ATTEMPT boundaries and debrief, executed as an announced, possession-granted tutor action (a 2–4 s 'let me grab a snapshot — one second' ritual driving Export XML via the command-set/AX channel with focus restore), and assign a demo-tier reliability target to that automation; (2) make AX-tree reads of the timeline (clip layout, selection, playhead — the surface CommandPost proves readable) the mid-attempt state proxy, demoting FCPXML to boundary-time ground truth; (3) restate the assessment section so live feedback during attempts is explicitly event-tape/AX-based and only debrief grading is FCPXML-based; (4) add 'no headless FCPXML export' to the risk register and an M0 exit gate for the checkpoint-export automation working on a stranger's Mac.


## [MAJOR] feasibility-2: The Watch-mode reliability gate (∏ ≥ 95%) is arithmetically incompatible with the plan's own demo shapes and its own T0 rule

**Argument:** Section 3.1 sets per-verified-step targets T2 ≥ 99.5%, T1 ≥ 97%, T0 ≥ 90%, and design-demonstration-control.md D4 defines the gate as 'predicted compound success (∏ of step targets) ≥95%.' Computing that product: a pure-T2 12-step demo — the plan's own canonical example ('a 12-step demo deliberately takes 2–4 minutes') — gives 0.995^12 ≈ 94.2%, below the gate. Pure T2 caps out at 10 steps (0.995^10 ≈ 95.1%). One T1 step (0.97) leaves room for only ~4 T2 steps (0.97 × 0.995^4 ≈ 95.0%), and the platform memo's '20-step scripted demo lands at ~1.5–3 minutes' is unreachable in Watch mode at any tier mix (0.995^20 ≈ 90.5%; 0.97^20 ≈ 54%). Worst, §3.3/D4 explicitly authorize 'T0 Watch segments capped at 5 steps' — but a SINGLE T0 step (0.90) already violates ∏ ≥ 95% (five give 0.9^5 ≈ 59%). As written, the gate forbids what the plan elsewhere promises, and engineering will hit the contradiction on day one of instrumenting it. The likely intent is post-retry success: with ≤2 retries plus tier-drop, effective per-step success is ≈ 1−(1−p)^3 (T1 ≈ 99.997%), under which 20-step demos pass trivially — but that is not what the document says.

**Suggested change:** Redefine the gate over post-recovery step success: 'a segment may run as a Watch demo only when predicted compound probability of every step VERIFYING within its retry/tier-drop budget is ≥95%' — and restate the T0 cap consistently (e.g., T0 steps allowed in Watch only where a T1/T2 verify channel exists, ≤5 per segment, because post-retry T0 ≈ 1−0.1^3 ≈ 99.9% only if failures are detectable and retryable). Alternatively, keep raw per-step targets but lower the Watch bar and delete the 12/20-step demo examples. Either way, make §3.1, §3.3, D4, and the platform memo's latency table use the same arithmetic.


## [MAJOR] feasibility-3: The $0 'continuous semantic layer' on FCP is an unvalidated assumption — and the entire foveated economics stands on it

**Argument:** Section 3.3 calls foveated observation 'the existence condition of the business' and prices it on a continuous layer of free AXObserver notifications ('learner selected Blade from the Trim menu — no model call'). But the CommandPost evidence cited proves FCP's AX tree is rich for READING and ACTING — CommandPost's own idiom is lazy element lookup and wait-for-element polling, not notification streams — it does not establish that FCP EMITS kAXValueChanged/kAXSelectedChildrenChanged notifications for the operations that constitute practice: timeline drags, trims, blade cuts, clip moves on a custom-drawn canvas. The plan's own documents concede the timeline canvas is AX-blind for verification ('vision-assert only where AX is blind, like FCP's viewer canvas' — design-demonstration D1 says 'the viewer/timeline canvas'). The headline example (a menu selection) is the easy case; most real editing is keystrokes (semantic only if you know the active command set and which pane has focus) and mouse edits (notification coverage unknown, likely sparse). If the tape is sparse, every downstream mechanism degrades: error-signature triggers don't fire, rubric checkpoints can't match 'expected state transitions,' the 80-look budget becomes the only eye, and the fallback — more vision — is exactly what the disqualified $26/hr architecture costs. No M0 exit gate or risk-register entry tests notification density.

**Suggested change:** Add a week-1 M0 spike with its own exit gate: instrument AXObserver against FCP and measure notification coverage for a scripted list of ~20 canonical editing actions (blade, ripple/roll drag, append, clip select, playhead move, tool switch). Define the fallback now: click-point AX hit-testing (AXUIElementCopyElementAtPosition on every tap-observed click) plus keystroke-to-command mapping via the installed command set, and state the revised look-budget if semantic coverage is below threshold. Add 'FCP AX notification density insufficient' to the risk register with the Guide-mode/checkpoint-assessment degradation named.


## [MAJOR] feasibility-4: M0/M1 schedule and headcount don't reconcile: the content pair doesn't exist in the stated team, and the calendar has zero slack

**Argument:** The plan assumes 'a founding team of 2–3 engineers plus the founder' (header) and prices M0 at '~37 engineer-weeks at 2.5 engineers' = 14.8 weeks — exactly the Sep 1 → mid-Dec window with zero slack for hiring ramp, holidays, or the 10 pilot sessions the exit gate requires (each on a 'stranger's Mac,' recruited and scheduled inside the same window). Simultaneously, §3.6 prices the 21-lesson course at 350–550 hours for 'one domain-expert + learning-engineer pair, about a quarter,' M0 says 'in parallel, the content pair starts authoring Units 1–3,' and M1 (Feb 2027) requires the course COMPLETE. That pair appears in no headcount: if the founder is the domain expert, the learning engineer must come out of the 2.5 engineers, dropping M0 to ~1.5–2 engineers and pushing 37 EW to 18–25 calendar weeks (M0 slips to Feb–Mar); if the pair is separate, the team is 4–5 people, contradicting the stated assumption. Worse, hand-authoring requires 'replay-test on a clean Mac' — impossible before the engine exists (mid-Dec at best) — so 21 lessons' worth of replay-testing and fixes compresses into the ~7 weeks between M0 exit and the Feb 2027 M1 launch, concurrent with running the first design-partner cohorts.

**Suggested change:** Pick one and state it: (a) add the content pair to the team assumption explicitly (founder as domain expert + a hired learning engineer, i.e., 3.5–4.5 people) and keep the dates; or (b) keep 2.5 engineers and move M0 exit to late Jan and M1 to Apr 2027. In either case, decouple 'M1 = design partners start' from 'course complete': launch cohort 1 on Units 1–3 (≈9 lessons) with the remaining units shipping during the design-partner period, and re-anchor the month-N commercial targets to the revised M1.


## [MINOR] feasibility-5: The prompt-cache mechanism as written fails on the plan's highest-frequency call paths: mid-conversation system messages are not supported on Sonnet 5

**Argument:** Section 2 states: 'volatile session state is injected as mid-conversation system messages so the prefix never invalidates,' and design-platform-core generalizes it to the whole model layer. Per current Anthropic API documentation, mid-conversation {role: "system"} messages are supported on Claude Opus 5 (and Opus 4.8/Fable/Mythos) but NOT on Claude Sonnet 5 — an unsupported model returns a 400 ('role system is not supported on this model'). The plan routes its highest-frequency calls through Sonnet 5: narration and feedback turns, and the default deep-look (up to 80/hour). On those paths the stated mechanism throws, and the naive fix — editing the top-level system prompt — is precisely the cache-buster the plan warns 'multiplies COGS ~6×.' The correct Sonnet-side pattern (volatile state as a <system-reminder> text block inside a user turn, appended after the cached prefix) has the same caching profile and is the documented fallback, but the plan presents the system-message injection as a verified, model-general mechanism when it is Opus-only.

**Suggested change:** Amend §2's model-layer paragraph: 'volatile session state is injected after the cached prefix — as mid-conversation system messages on Opus 5, and as <system-reminder> blocks in user turns on Sonnet 5/Haiku (role:system is Opus-only) — so the frozen lesson-pack prefix never invalidates.' Add a CI assertion that per-model request builders never touch the cached prefix (the cache-discipline CI the plan already promises).


## [MINOR] feasibility-6: Deep-look and verification screenshots will contain the tutor's own overlay unless explicitly excluded — nothing in the plan excludes it

**Argument:** The step lifecycle keeps the spotlight on screen from TELEGRAPH through ACT ('nothing is ever touched that wasn't first highlighted'), and VERIFY's screenshot+zoom fallback fires immediately after ACT — while the overlay is still dimming everything but the target at 0.45 and drawing captions/Set-of-Marks labels. Likewise the Haiku pre-screen (1 frame/5 s during practice) and the six-trigger deep looks will capture telestration, possession badges, and hint arrows whenever they're visible. A vision model judging 'did the postcondition hold' or 'is the learner off-path' from a 55%-dimmed, annotated frame is degraded in exactly the moments verification matters, and Set-of-Marks labels in observation frames can confuse grounding. ScreenCaptureKit's SCContentFilter supports excluding an application's windows from capture, so the fix is one architectural sentence — but no document in the plan or the five design memos mentions it, and it is the kind of silent quality bug that would surface as 'T0 verification is mysteriously unreliable' months in.

**Suggested change:** Add to §2 (ScreenCaptureKit pipeline): 'All capture paths exclude the shell's own overlay windows via SCContentFilter app/window exclusion; screenshots show the learner's screen as if the tutor were not drawing on it. Where a verify step needs to confirm overlay-relative state (e.g., Set-of-Marks grounding for T0), a separate composited capture mode is used deliberately.' Add a CI test that captured frames contain no overlay pixels.


## [MINOR] feasibility-7: The FCP command-set channel needs a spec: installing/activating the tutor's set is itself GUI automation, and it clobbers learner customizations

**Argument:** T2 for FCP is 'an installed known command set driven by keystroke injection.' Three unstated realities: (1) FCP has ONE active command set — the tutor's synthetic keystrokes and the learner's real keystrokes route through the same table, so if the tutor's set isn't a strict superset of the default, the demo teaches '⌘B blades the clip' while the active mappings differ from what the learner will live with after the session, violating P1's transfer premise; (2) editors who customized their shortcuts (exactly the FCP.cafe/CommandPost power users §6 targets) get their set deactivated or shadowed during sessions — restoring it on exit and on crash must be specified or the first pro user with custom keys experiences a trust incident; (3) switching command sets is done via FCP's Commands menu — itself AX automation that needs possession and a verify step, and command-set files are version-pinned per FCP release (the plan notes fragility but not the activation mechanics). Additionally FCP shortcuts are focus-context-sensitive (timeline vs browser vs viewer), so T2 preconditions must include pane focus, not just frontmost-app.

**Suggested change:** Add a short spec paragraph to §3.1's T2 bullet: the tutor's command set is the FCP default plus automation bindings on otherwise-unused keys (F13–F19/hyper combos); it is installed at onboarding and activated/deactivated via a verified AX menu action at session start/end, with restoration of the learner's prior set guaranteed on exit and on crash recovery (journaled); onboarding detects a customized learner set and asks before shadowing it; every T2 keystroke step's precondition includes the required focused pane, verified via AX before posting.



# REVIEWER: constitution

## overall
This is an unusually constitution-faithful plan — the capability ladder, possession protocol, artifact-first assessment, and the assist-metric force-cap genuinely operationalize the inversion, and most strains are admitted in writing; every load-bearing number I re-derived against the research memos reconciles. But it fails its own honesty standard at three points: the trust story is internally contradicted by the continuous cloud pre-screen (in the one dimension the constitution calls concept-level and the risk register calls existential), the hour-meter's revenue incentive against the fade is never confronted, and two constitutional decisions — the guided-only pivot and the canonical scenario's dropped agency beat — are made silently at plan level. As amended by these findings I would stake money on it; as written, the §9 P7 "None" row alone would eventually surface as a broken promise to learners.


## [BLOCKER] constitution-1: The trust posture contradicts its own architecture: 'screenshots only on six named triggers' and the P7 'None' row are false while the Haiku pre-screen streams the learner's screen to the cloud every 5 seconds

**Argument:** The §0 decision table states 'screenshots only on six named triggers, hard cap 80 deep looks/hour', and §9's P7 row claims 'None' — zero strain. But §3.3 itself specifies 'a Haiku pre-screen (one frame per 5 s) runs only during active practice' — that is ~720 frames/hour of the learner's screen uploaded to a cloud model during practice, on top of the 80 deep looks. The design memo compounds it: 'budget enforcement is also a privacy property: the tutor provably looks rarely' (design-observation-assessment.md §1). It does not look rarely; it looks every five seconds while the learner practices. The constitution makes trust 'a concept-level requirement, not a feature' (P7), and the plan's own risk register (#8) declares 'any breach of stated posture is treated as existential.' The stated posture (§0 table, §4 'ephemeral… looks rarely' narrative, the one-page data map framing) is breached by the plan's own architecture before a single learner signs up. If the trust copy ships as written, learners are materially misled in the product's most sensitive dimension; if the copy is corrected, the 'provably looks rarely' positioning collapses — either way §9's P7 'None' is untrue as written.

**Suggested change:** Reconcile in one of two directions and make every surface consistent: (a) make the practice-time pre-screen local-only (small on-device model or AX/event heuristics generating triggers), so 'cloud screenshots only on six triggers' becomes true, accepting weaker anomaly detection until the Architecture-C local model lands; or (b) keep the Haiku pre-screen and rewrite the §0 Observation row, §4 privacy narrative, the data map, and §9's P7 row to say plainly: 'during active practice a low-tier model sees one frame per 5 s; frontier deep looks are capped at 80/hour' — and surface pre-screen frame counts in the 'What your tutor did' log. Delete every 'looks rarely' claim that option (b) falsifies.


## [MAJOR] constitution-2: Metered tutor-hours create a direct conflict of interest with the inversion, and the plan never faces it: no metric guards hours-to-competency, and the meter taxes the constitutional core while the 'What it is not' surface is free

**Argument:** Revenue is tutor-hours consumed ($49/5h, $89/10h, $12/h top-ups): the company earns more the longer a learner needs live tutoring. The inversion demands the opposite — 'the human ends up more capable, not less.' The assist-per-task metric (§3.7) guards only help-intensity within a task; it says nothing about hours. Pace factor, time-boxes, narration density, remediation branches, and review scheduling are all engine-controlled dials (design-curriculum-adaptation §5) where every marginal hour is $8–12 of revenue — a tutor could keep assist slopes beautifully negative while teaching slowly, and no dashboard metric (§7) would notice. §6 'the churn truth, faced' faces graduation churn but never this per-hour incentive. Second edge of the same blade: the meter prices exactly what the constitution calls the product ('this loop, running live… is the product' — live demonstration and observed practice) at $12/h, while the surface the constitution excludes ('Not a chatbot that can only talk about the skill without seeing or touching it') is 'unmetered' and free — cost-sensitive learners are steered toward the blind chat and away from observed practice.

**Suggested change:** Add a companion anti-dependence metric to §3.7 and the §7 dashboard: median tutor-hours-to-capstone (or hours per mastery-point gained) per cohort, published alongside completion rates, with a stated band and a trend requirement. Add one commitment sentence to §5: pacing, time-boxes, and review scheduling are never tuned against revenue, and unused rolled-over hours convert to alumni-mode credit at graduation. Note explicitly in §9 (P4 row or a new row) that metered pricing structurally rewards slower teaching and name the hours metric as the guard, exactly as the assist force-cap guards scaffolding.


## [MAJOR] constitution-3: Kill-criterion 1 authorizes an unconstitutional product from inside the execution plan

**Argument:** The plan's own status line says 'the loop, the principles, and the inversion are not [changeable].' The constitution: 'explain → demonstrate → learner attempts → observe → feedback → adapt… This loop… *is* the product,' and its closing instruction permits changing execution freely 'but the loop, the principles, and the inversion are the product.' Kill-criterion 1 (§7) pre-authorizes 'pivot to guided-only (no autonomous demonstration),' which deletes the demonstrate stage and P2 outright. Naming it 'a pivot, never a quiet degradation' is honest about visibility but dishonest about authority: an execution plan proposed *against* the constitution cannot grant itself the right to ship a product the constitution forbids. Guided-only is not a pivot of this product; it is a different product requiring a rewritten concept brief.

**Suggested change:** Reword criterion 1's consequence: 'Unassisted session completion <60% at commercial month 4 after two architecture iterations → stop and escalate to the constitution's owner. Guided-only abandons the teaching loop's demonstrate stage and P2; it requires the founder to amend or rewrite the concept brief before any such product ships — this plan cannot authorize it.' Mirror the same wording in §9's P2 row.


## [MAJOR] constitution-4: The canonical scenario's agency beat is dropped — the tutor no longer asks where to begin theory — and the board's constitutional assessment role vanishes; neither loss is admitted in §9

**Argument:** Constitution: 'The tutor asks where to begin with theory and fundamentals, given the apps available on his machine — Figma, for example. Anup says Figma.' The learner chooses the blackboard. Plan §1: 'if Figma is installed, the tutor opens it… otherwise its own board window, silently'; §3.5's selection rule is pure detection (installed ∧ handshake <5 s ∧ not opted out) — the ask is gone, replaced by auto-selection with a buried opt-out. Additionally, the constitution twice assigns the board an assessment role ('uses that same surface for assessment'; 'for explanation and for assessment'), and the plan's fixed assessment order (artifact → fluency → verbal, §3.3) contains no board-based assessment anywhere. Both are defensible engineering simplifications, but §9's P1 row admits only the Figma-less-machine dilution — the compliance table is silent on the lost ask and the lost assessment surface, which is precisely the quiet dilution of the signature move the constitution's scenario exists to prevent.

**Suggested change:** In §3.5 and §1: on first session the tutor names the detected candidate surfaces and asks aloud ('I can teach theory in your Figma, or on my own board — where should we set up?'), then remembers the preference. Add lightweight board-based retrieval checks to the Board IR (learner arranges/labels a diagram; graded via the Figma node tree or built-in board state — the same artifact-first machinery), or, if deferred, add both omissions honestly to §9's P1 and P5 rows.


## [MINOR] constitution-5: 'Language policy is measured, not asked' over-extends P5's distrust of self-report from capability to preference, against P4 and P7

**Argument:** §3.4: 'Language policy is measured, not asked' — the tutor mirrors the observed code-switch ratio ±10 points. The constitution licenses distrust of self-report only for capability ('capability honestly assessed from what the learner actually does, not what they claim' — P5); language is a P4 personalization dimension, and P7 gives the learner control of the session. A learner who says 'English only, please — I'm preparing for an English-speaking studio' would be fought by the mirroring policy as written. Preferences are not claims; measurement should set the default, not override a stated request.

**Suggested change:** Amend §3.4 to 'measured by default, overridden on request': an explicit learner language request always wins and persists in the learner model (e.g., lang.override); observed-ratio mirroring applies only absent a stated preference.


## [MINOR] constitution-6: The feedback-queueing policy strains the constitution's 'real-time feedback… mistakes spotted the moment they happen' — pedagogically defensible, but unadmitted in §9, and the learner-adjustable struggle-timer dial was dropped from the plan

**Argument:** Constitution ('What the tutor is'): 'Real-time feedback as the learner works: mistakes spotted the moment they happen.' Plan §3.3: default silence, no unsolicited help before 45 s of struggle, two interruptions per attempt, everything else queued to the debrief. The deliberate-practice justification is sound, but it is a real reinterpretation of the constitution's text, and §9's P3/P5 rows admit only the look budget and blind spots — this behavioral strain is not in the table, which the plan's own honesty standard requires. Separately, design-observation-assessment §4 makes the struggle timer 'a per-learner preference dial with a floor of 20s' (a P4 mitigation), and the plan silently dropped the dial.

**Suggested change:** Add to §9 (row 3 or 5): 'The 45-s struggle timer and 2-interruption budget deliberately delay feedback the constitution words as immediate; accepted on deliberate-practice evidence, bounded by the immediate-interrupt whitelist.' Restore the per-learner timer dial (floor 20 s) in §3.3.


## [MINOR] constitution-7: Kill-criterion 2 gates on a 'trial→paid' conversion the plan's pricing never defines

**Argument:** §7 criterion 2: 'Week-4 retention <25% or trial→paid <8% at month 6.' §5 and §6 define no product trial — the funnel is an application-gated waitlist admitted into paid cohorts at $39/$49 ('FCP trial users' refers to Apple's 90-day FCP trial, not a tutor trial). As written, the criterion is unmeasurable: at month 6 nobody will be able to say whether it tripped, which defeats the stated purpose of writing kill criteria down 'so nobody re-litigates them under stress.'

**Suggested change:** Either define trial mechanics in §5 (e.g., one free live assessment session for waitlist admits — which §6's community playbook already gives away — with trial→paid measured from it), or restate criterion 2 in terms that exist: cohort-offer→paid conversion <8%.


## [MINOR] constitution-8: The B2B chapter has no constitutional guardrail on who owns the learner's data — deferral is not an answer to the vendor-widget clause

**Argument:** Constitution: 'it is the *learner's* tutor' and P7 gives the learner control 'of their data.' §6 defers B2B but arms pull-forward triggers that can fire by commercial month 6 and starts SOC 2 groundwork regardless — yet says nothing about what an employer buyer may see. Enterprise training buyers expect per-employee assessment dashboards; a screen-observing tutor whose honest assessments flow to the learner's manager becomes the buyer's observation instrument — the 'not an in-app help widget owned by one software vendor' failure mode entering through the employer door instead of the software vendor's. §9's 'Not a vendor widget' row answers only with 'B2B deferred,' which expires the moment a trigger fires.

**Suggested change:** Add one commitment sentence to §6: in any B2B deployment, session observations and the longitudinal learner model belong to the learner; employers receive only completion/competency attestations on terms the learner sees and accepts — written into the SOC 2 scope and sales collateral before the first pilot, and added to §9's vendor-widget row.



# REVIEWER: market

## overall
I would not stake money on this plan as written: its wedge is anchored to a trial mechanic Apple killed seven months before the plan's "verified" date (Creator Studio, Jan 2026, $12.99/mo, 30-day trial), its month-12 milestone requires ~800-900 gross paid adds/month that its own organic, cohort-gated channels cannot produce — with a kill criterion that then mandates shutdown — and it never says who funds twelve-plus months of negative contribution margin. The engineering and COGS sections are unusually rigorous and largely survive attack (Wyzant rates, margin arithmetic, and the foveated-observation math all check out), so this is a salvageable plan, not a dead one. As amended by findings 1-3 — re-wedged on Creator Studio's subscriber inflow, targets cut ~60-70% to match channel capacity, and a financing section added — I would consider it fundable at seed.


## [BLOCKER] market-1: The wedge's anchor fact is dead: Apple killed the 90-day FCP trial in January 2026 and now sells FCP for $12.99/mo

**Argument:** The plan's primary audience and native offer — 'FCP costs $299.99 with a 90-day free trial. Every trial user is on a purchase-decision countdown — get good enough in 90 days to justify $300' (§1, §0 table, design-gtm §1) — is built on a mechanic Apple discontinued seven months before the plan's 'verified 2026-08-02' date. Apple launched Apple Creator Studio on Jan 13, 2026 (live Jan 28): FCP + Logic Pro + Pixelmator Pro + Motion at $12.99/mo or $129/yr, with a 30-day trial replacing the standalone 90-day trials (Apple Newsroom 2026-01-13; 9to5Mac 2026-01-19; Bloomberg; Variety). Three load-bearing claims break: (a) the '90-day clock' segment no longer exists — the clock is 30 days and there is still no channel to reach trial users anyway; (b) the '$300 justify-the-purchase' WTP logic evaporates when the software costs $12.99/mo — and the optics of a $49/mo tutor for a $12.99/mo app are brutal; (c) 'FCP is the copilot-free zone; Apple ships no in-app assistant' is now weakening — Creator Studio shipped AI features inside FCP (Visual Search, Beat Detection), direct evidence Apple is investing in AI inside FCP, raising risk-register rows 6 and 7. The whole §1 'self-selected by money and a clock' rationale, the price-integrity paragraph in §5, and the M1/M2 offer copy must be rebuilt.

**Suggested change:** Rewrite §1's audience rationale and §5's price-integrity paragraph around the post-January-2026 reality: segment (a) becomes Creator Studio 30-day trialists and new $12.99/mo subscribers (a larger, lower-friction inflow — the silver lining worth claiming); reframe the offer as 'be genuinely competent before your first renewal cycle'; address the $49-tutor-vs-$12.99-software optic head-on ('the software is cheap; competence is what costs'); update the §0 table row and the 'copilot-free zone' claim to acknowledge Apple's in-FCP AI features; and re-verify every §1 market fact, since the document's 'verified 2026-08-02' stamp demonstrably failed here.


## [BLOCKER] market-2: The month-12 growth target requires ~800–900 gross paid adds/month organic-only — the stated channels and cohort mechanics cannot produce a tenth of that, and kill criterion 4 then mandates shutdown

**Argument:** Recomputed against the plan's own numbers: going from 500 payers (month 6) to 4,000 payers (month 12) at the plan's stated 10–15% monthly churn requires 797–918 gross paid additions per month, every month, in months 7–12 (at 12.5% churn: 856/mo). That is 28–42 cohorts of 20–30 per month — more than one cohort admitted per day — against §6's own mechanic of application-gated cohorts of 20–30 'so demo reliability and observation load stay debuggable,' with zero paid acquisition. At the plan's own trial→paid floor of 8%, it implies ~10,000+ activated Mac-owning FCP-learner trials per month, organic, from a community the plan itself concedes is 'much smaller' than r/davinciresolve's ~221k (a stale pre-Sept-2025 figure; Reddit no longer publishes counts). Even the entire FCP subreddit plus FCP.cafe plus film schools is tens of thousands of people; the target implies converting several percent of the entire worldwide reachable community into $49/mo payers within one year of a category nobody has heard of. Worse, kill criterion 4 ('MRR <$100k at month 12 → domain pivot or shutdown') is calibrated to this unreachable ramp: the plan hard-wires its own pivot/shutdown even in the scenario where the product works and grows at an excellent-for-organic rate.

**Suggested change:** Reset the month-12 milestone to what the stated channels can plausibly produce: ~1,200–1,500 payers / $50–70k MRR (≈150–250 gross adds/month at peak, still aggressive for organic), and recalibrate kill criterion 4 to ~$40–50k MRR at month 12. Alternatively, keep 4,000 but then the plan must name the channel that fills the gap (paid acquisition post-referral-gate, B2B pull-forward, or a second domain earlier) and drop the cohort-gating assumption from §6 with a stated date. Add the arithmetic (adds/month required at stated churn) to §7 so the target and the channel model are visibly consistent.


## [MAJOR] market-3: No financing section: at launch margins the plan loses money on every customer for ~12 months and never states runway, burn, or a raise

**Argument:** The plan's own numbers: launch GM 10–18% under Architecture B means contribution per customer of $33–88 over a full customer lifetime ($49 ARPU × 6.7–10 months × 10–18%), below the plan's own blended CAC <$100 ceiling — i.e., negative unit economics after any nonzero CAC for the entire B phase, before payroll. Meanwhile the plan commits 2.5 engineers + founder for ~18 months (Sep 2026 → Feb 2028), a content pair for 350–550 authoring hours, concierge onboarding, weekly design-partner calls, SOC 2 groundwork, and COGS that at 500 users × ~4 h/mo is ~$16k/mo — a fully-loaded spend on the order of $1M+ before month-12 revenue is material even if targets are hit. The entire margin path 'runs through architecture C and token deflation,' which the plan itself flags as a thesis that can fail (kill criterion 3). Yet the word 'raise,' 'runway,' or 'funding' appears nowhere. An execution plan whose economics are explicitly transitional-negative must say who pays for the transition; as written it is not executable by a bootstrapped team and silently assumes a seed round it never plans for.

**Suggested change:** Add a §5.1 'Financing and runway': estimated monthly burn by phase (M0, M1, M2–M3), cumulative cash need to month 12 under the base and miss scenarios, the explicit statement that launch unit economics are CAC-negative until B→C lands, and the funding plan (raise size/timing, or the revenue/cost configuration that makes the team default-alive). Tie kill criterion 3 (deflation thesis failed) to a concrete cash consequence.


## [MAJOR] market-4: The free-good-enough response is unpriced: Gemini Live screen-share + Guided Learning already exist, and the plan's own architecture concedes Guide mode is legitimate teaching

**Argument:** The plan's §3.1 states that T0-only apps 'default to Guide-mode teaching (tutor narrates and overlays; learner drives)' — i.e., the plan itself certifies guidance-without-demonstration as an acceptable teaching mode. But that is approximately what Gemini Live screen-share (free, shipping, verified in the plan's own research) plus Guided Learning (shipped Aug 2025) already deliver on any app with zero install, zero scary TCC prompts, and zero dollars. Fusing them is a product-flag flip for Google, not a research program. The 'empty corridor between content subs and human tutors' pricing argument ignores the $0 shelf directly beneath it. What genuinely survives a free fusion is demonstration-by-acting, artifact-graded assessment, the adapter library, and longitudinal memory — but risk-register row 5's containment is only 'moat in curriculum/adapters/learner model/trust; move fast on category,' with no concrete week-one playbook, no leading indicator, and no kill-criteria linkage. For a $49/mo consumer product, the week Google ships this free is a demand-collapse event the plan should have a named response to, not a table cell.

**Suggested change:** Add a 'competitive response playbook' to §6 or §8: (a) leading indicator — exit-survey mentions of free screen-aware tools and trial→paid trend, reviewed monthly; (b) day-one response if a lab ships fused screen-share tutoring free — reposition on the three things guidance cannot do (demonstrate in your app, grade your actual FCPXML, remember your skill graph), publish completion/assessment outcome data as the proof asset, and accelerate the B2B pull-forward (enterprises won't run learner screens through a free consumer Google product); (c) a pricing contingency (e.g., unmetered-chat tier becomes free-with-assessment-teaser). State explicitly in §5 that the price is defended by demonstration + assessment + memory, not by screen-watching, which is already free.


## [MINOR] market-5: LTV and 'payers' arithmetic is internally inconsistent: $390 gross assumes every payer is a full-price Core subscriber for their whole life, while the plan's own tiers say otherwise

**Argument:** LTV ≈ $49 × 8 ≈ $390 gross (§6, design-gtm §4) assumes $49 ARPU across the full paid life. But the first 100 customers pay $39 (locked 12 months), graduates are pushed to $9/mo alumni mode, and the multi-skill roadmap that would restore ARPU (Resolve) only enters beta in H2 2027. At 15% churn the same formula gives $327 even at full price. Meanwhile the month-12 claim '4,000 payers, $150–200k MRR' implies $37.50–50 ARPU, which requires nearly all 4,000 to be fresh full-price subscribers within their first 3–4 months — yet with a 3–4 month competency arc and graduation-as-success, a large share of cumulative customers at month 12 should be $9 alumni. The plan never defines whether 'payers' includes alumni. Every one of these numbers feeds the CAC<$100 gate, so the ambiguity propagates into the funnel economics.

**Suggested change:** Define 'payer' (segment: Core/Serious active vs founding vs alumni) and restate the month-6/month-12 targets per segment. Restate LTV as a blended cohort figure (e.g., 4 months Core + N months alumni at stated churn per phase) with the resulting gross and contribution numbers, and carry that blended figure into the CAC ceiling. Sync design-gtm.md's stale milestone dates (Oct 2026/Feb 2027/Aug 2027) with the plan's re-based clock so the two documents stop disagreeing.


## [MINOR] market-6: The Nepali cohort is never costed and its Kathmandu-side willingness-to-pay is never examined

**Argument:** The plan calls the ~30-learner Nepali-English cohort 'a signature, not a side quest' but assigns it no engineering budget and no pricing analysis. Costs it actually carries for a 2.5-engineer team: Sarvam Saaras integration, Gemini Flash TTS integration, the hands-on Nepali TTS validation gate (risk 11), the code-switch mirroring policy (§3.4), Nepali-language support and QA of narration — realistically several engineer-weeks landing exactly at M2 (~May 2027), when the same team must scale open cohorts. On revenue: $39–49/mo is a serious ask against Nepal's income levels for students who must also own a Mac and FCP/Creator Studio; the plausible payers are diaspora creators, whom the plan mentions but does not prioritize. The marketing claim cuts both ways: 'no US competitor will bother to copy' also means the asset has limited pull in the market where the payers are. It is defensible as principle-4 proof and founder signature — but only if budgeted and kept off the critical path.

**Suggested change:** Add a one-paragraph honest costing to §6: engineer-week budget cap (e.g., ≤4 weeks integration + validation), diaspora-first recruitment (payers with US/UK/AU incomes) with Kathmandu students on a subsidized founding rate treated as marketing spend, and an explicit statement that the cohort ships only after M2's open-cohort reliability gates are green — it contributes zero to the month-6 payer target and must not block it.



# REVIEWER: trust-safety

## overall
The below-the-model trust architecture (possession lease, hardware interrupt, session scoping, action log) is genuinely the strongest part of this plan, and the Figma-bridge and FCP command-set legal posture check out. But I would not sign off as written: the plan's absolute privacy language is contradicted by the actual Aug-2026 terms of the vendors it selects (Deepgram trains on audio by default; Anthropic retains frames 7 days, 2 years if flagged), and three mechanical holes — the unscoped input tap, the frontmost-check TOCTOU race, and zero containment for in-whitelist destruction — are exactly the gaps a hostile journalist or a bad week would find. All are fixable with the amendments above, most in days not months; with findings 1–4 applied I would stake money on this plan, because the remaining risks are execution risks, not architecture lies.


## [BLOCKER] trust-safety-1: The privacy promise is contradicted by the vendor terms the plan itself selects — Deepgram trains on mic audio by default, and Anthropic retains screenshots for 7 days (2 years if flagged)

**Argument:** The plan promises 'screenshots are ephemeral… never written to disk, never used for training' and a data map saying 'nothing leaves the machine except model API calls' (§4; design-platform-core §7). Verified against live sources 2026-08-02: (a) Anthropic's standard API retains inputs and outputs — including every screenshot — for 7 days (reduced from 30 in Sept 2025), and content flagged by its automated trust-and-safety systems for up to 2 years ([Anthropic retention docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention), [Anthropic Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)). The plan's own architecture routes computer-use screenshots through Anthropic's injection classifiers — an explicit flagging pathway into that 2-year bucket. Zero-data-retention agreements exist but are enterprise-qualifying ([ZDR article](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8956058-i-have-a-zero-data-retention-agreement-with-anthropic-what-products-does-it-apply-to)) — a pre-revenue startup will not have one at M0. (b) Deepgram's pay-as-you-go default enrolls customers in the Model Improvement Partnership — customer audio IS used for training unless each request carries mip_opt_out=true, and opting out forfeits the ~50% MIP discount, changing the quoted $0.0077/min COGS basis ([Deepgram MIP docs](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/the-deepgram-model-improvement-partnership-program)). As spec'd, continuous learner mic audio would be training data — a flat contradiction of the stated posture. (c) Gemini paid tier does not train on prompts but logs them 55 days for abuse monitoring ([Google usage policies](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/usage-policies)). (d) Sarvam's privacy policy mentions using data to 'train and refine AI models'; its API data terms are unverified ([sarvam.ai/privacy-policy](https://www.sarvam.ai/privacy-policy)) — yet risk #11 gates the Nepali cohort only on voice QUALITY, not on data terms. Risk #8 declares 'any breach of stated posture is existential' — the current absolute language manufactures that breach on day one.

**Suggested change:** Rewrite §4's privacy paragraph to claim only what is true: frames are ephemeral ON THE LEARNER'S MACHINE; expand the one-page data map into a per-vendor table (vendor, data sent, retention window, training use, processing region) covering Anthropic (7-day default, up to 2-year T&S-flag retention, no training), STT vendor, TTS vendor. Mandate mip_opt_out=true (or a negotiated no-training DPA) for Deepgram and re-run the voice COGS line at non-MIP rates in §5. Add an M1 gate: signed DPAs/no-training terms with every voice vendor; pursue Anthropic ZDR and name it as roadmap, not current fact. Add Sarvam data-terms verification to risk #11's validation gate alongside voice quality.


## [MAJOR] trust-safety-2: The Input Monitoring tap is a session-long global keylogger — the app blocklist covers capture and AX but never gates the event tap, and keystroke data rides to the cloud in the event tape

**Argument:** The blocklist is enforced 'at the capture-filter and AX-attach level' (§4; design-observation-assessment §5) — but the CGEventTap is a third channel, and nothing in the plan or design memos scopes it to whitelisted apps. During a session, a learner who cmd-tabs to Messages to answer a text, types a 2FA code into a browser login, or uses a password manager's quick-access panel outside a SecureEventInput field has those keystrokes captured by the tap. Worse, the deep-look pipeline sends 'the recent event tape' to Anthropic as context (design-observation-assessment §1), so captured keystroke content can literally leave the machine inside model calls. SecureEventInput protects password FIELDS only — the plan's trust copy ('password field — I can't and won't see this') implies a boundary far broader than the mechanism provides. The worst realistic headline — 'AI tutor app logged users' private messages and 2FA codes and sent them to a cloud AI' — is currently prevented by nothing but the learner never leaving FCP. Session-scoping bounds WHEN the tap exists, not WHAT it swallows while it exists.

**Suggested change:** Add to §3.2/§4 a hard rule enforced in the Swift tap callback: keystroke and click events are DROPPED at the tap (not merely unanalyzed) whenever the frontmost app is not on the session whitelist — at most an activity-present boolean is retained; raw keystroke text from non-whitelisted apps never enters the session tape. Additionally: cloud-bound event-tape excerpts carry semantic command events only (menu/command names), never raw typed character streams; state both rules in the data map. Reword the SecureEventInput copy to name it as one layer, with the frontmost-gate as the actual boundary.


## [MAJOR] trust-safety-3: Frontmost-check-then-post is a TOCTOU race — a focus steal between check and delivery lands tutor keystrokes in the wrong app; the fix (PID-targeted posting) is absent

**Argument:** §3.2 enforces the whitelist by checking the frontmost app 'before every synthetic event.' CGEventPost delivers system-wide to whatever is frontmost AT DELIVERY, not at check time. A notification banner grabbing key focus, a Space switch, an app throwing a dialog, or the learner cmd-tabbing in the gap between check and post delivers the event elsewhere. The demo channel is largely keystroke injection into an installed FCP command set — chords like Cmd-Delete or Cmd-S delivered into Finder, Mail, or a save dialog are destructive, and the ≤100ms interrupt guarantee only fires on the learner's HARDWARE input, not on focus changes caused by other software. macOS provides the structural fix — CGEventPostToPid targets a specific process regardless of focus — and CommandPost's own channel proves per-app delivery works for FCP. The plan's 'stray dialog… hard-pauses and asks' handles the detected case; it does not close the race window itself.

**Suggested change:** Amend §3.2's scope bullet: all synthetic keyboard events are posted via CGEventPostToPid to the whitelisted target app's PID, never system-wide; mouse events re-verify the target element's owning window via AX immediately before delivery; any kAXApplicationActivated / focus-change notification aborts the in-flight synthetic queue the same way hardware input does. Add 'wrong-app delivery' to the risk register with this containment.


## [MAJOR] trust-safety-4: The whitelist scopes WHERE the tutor acts, not WHAT it may do — in-whitelist destruction (the learner's own FCP library) has no containment, no protected paths, no undo ledger

**Argument:** Deleting the learner's FCP library, trashing their media, or overwriting their export are all fully in-scope actions inside whitelisted FCP. The immediate-interrupt list covers destructive actions BY THE LEARNER (§3.3); nothing bounds the tutor's own verbs. Two model-driven paths can act semi-autonomously inside the whitelist: step repair and T0 recovery — and risk #12's injection containment ('whitelist below the model') is inert against screen content steering the model toward in-scope destruction (e.g., text visible inside a media clip in FCP's viewer, which is exactly the surface the plan admits AX cannot see and screenshots must read). An interrupted drag posts mouse-up 'where it is' and marks the step failed (design-demonstration-control D3) — honest, but the file has still been dropped somewhere; verification detects, it does not restore. §9 claims P7 is strained 'nowhere'; this is the somewhere. The plan already has the ingredients (starter .fcpbundle assets, FCPXML snapshots, action log) but never assembles them into a machine-safety story.

**Suggested change:** Add a 'containment inside the whitelist' block to §3.2: (1) sessions run in a dedicated tutor-created FCP library; the tutor never operates while the learner's personal library is the open target; (2) an executor-level forbidden-verb list (delete library/event, move-to-trash of media, empty trash, overwrite-without-copy, close-without-save) that requires explicit learner confirmation in every possession mode, enforced in the shell like the whitelist; (3) a pre-step FCPXML/file snapshot before any destructive-class step — an undo ledger the RECOVERY state can restore from; (4) filesystem writes confined to the lesson folder (protected-paths). Extend risk #12 to name in-whitelist injection-steered destruction, contained by (2) and (3).


## [MAJOR] trust-safety-5: Recording learners for YouTube, the Nepali cohort, and EU diaspora users create concrete legal obligations the plan never mentions — no consent mechanics, no GDPR/DPA posture, no cross-border story, age gate is self-attestation only

**Argument:** §6 makes weekly published recordings of real learners (screen + voice + working style) the PRIMARY channel, with consent handled by one waitlist checkbox ('willingness to be recorded' — design-gtm §5). A published session can expose filenames, notifications, faces in footage, and the learner's assessed struggle — consent must be specific, separable from product access, revocable pre-publication, and paired with a redaction pass; none of this exists. The Nepali cohort's voice and behavioral data flows to US and India-based processors; Nepal's Individual Privacy Act 2018 (2075) requires consent for collection and safeguards for personal data leaving Nepal ([Pioneer Law summary](https://pioneerlaw.com/individual-privacy-act-2018-2075/), [lawalpine guide](https://lawalpine.com/blog/privacy-laws-in-nepal)). 'Diaspora creators' includes EU/UK residents, which triggers GDPR: lawful basis, processor DPAs with every vendor (see finding 1), SCCs, deletion rights that must reach synced learner-model data — and counsel should assess whether the frustration-detection feature ('shifts 20 points toward the primary language when frustration runs high', §3.4) touches the EU AI Act's emotion-recognition provisions for education contexts, in force since 2025. Finally, '18+ launch policy' has no enforcement beyond implication; the target audience (film students, CapCut graduates) skews young, and a screen-observing input-monitoring product with minors aboard via a lie is exactly the incident risk #8 calls existential. The plan budgets SOC 2 but zero consumer-privacy compliance work.

**Suggested change:** Add a compliance workstream to §7 with M1 as the deadline: (a) recording consent as a separate, revocable, review-before-publish agreement with a mandatory redaction pass and withdrawal window; (b) GDPR readiness — vendor DPA stack, privacy notice, data-subject deletion covering cloud-synced learner models; counsel opinion on the EU AI Act emotion-inference question before serving EU learners; (c) Nepali-cohort consent documents covering cross-border transfer per the Privacy Act 2075, gating M2's cohort launch; (d) age gate = 18+ attestation + payment card + the existing application review, with under-18 applications rejected explicitly. Add one register row: 'compliance gap (recording consent / GDPR / Nepal cross-border / minors)'.


## [MINOR] trust-safety-6: No duty-of-care protocol: the tutor is built to detect frustration and read screens for hours, but has no defined behavior for encountering distress or concerning content

**Argument:** The product observes adults under stress by design — frustration spikes are a first-class signal (§3.4, §3.7), sessions run 60+ minutes, and deep looks read whatever is in frame in the practice app. Nothing defines what the tutor does when the learner voices despair ('what's the point, I'm too stupid for this'), when a deep look catches concerning material inside in-scope content (footage being edited can contain anything), or when frustration crosses from pedagogical signal to human concern. An improvised model response in that moment is a reputational coin-flip, and 'the tutor kept coaching while the user spiraled' is a category-poisoning story for a product whose brand is a caring 1:1 relationship. This is cheap to specify and expensive to improvise.

**Suggested change:** Add a short duty-of-care subsection to §4: scripted, bounded responses for verbal distress cues (acknowledge, no diagnosis, offer to pause the session, signpost to help resources once — never nag); concerning content seen in-frame is never stored, never assessed, and never surfaced beyond a neutral offer to stop; frustration data is used only for pacing/language adaptation and is named in the data map. Wire these as fixed policy in the system prompt and test them in the seeded-error QA suite.

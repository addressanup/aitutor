# Concept brief: the live AI tutor

## The idea in one line

An AI agent that lives on the learner's computer and uses full computer-use capability — screen, mouse, keyboard, voice — not to do tasks *for* them, but to *teach* them: a one-to-one tutor that explains, demonstrates live in real software, watches the learner practice, and adapts continuously to their pace, language, and ability.

## The inversion

Every AI agent on the market today points its capability in one direction: doing the work so the human doesn't have to. This product points the same capability the opposite way. The agent's ability to see the screen and operate the machine is used the way a great teacher uses their hands — to show, to guide, to observe — so that the *human* ends up more capable, not less. Agents everywhere else replace effort. This one builds skill.

## What the tutor is

A Jarvis-like presence on the learner's machine that teaches the way the best human tutors do — through voice, text, and live on-screen action, all at once.

It **explains** fundamentals and theory first, using whatever surface serves the lesson — including creatively repurposing the apps already installed on the learner's computer as its blackboard. If Figma is there, the tutor opens it, creates a workspace, and teaches on it the way a professor uses a whiteboard — diagrams, structure, worked concepts — and uses that same surface for assessment.

It **demonstrates** for real. When it's time for practice, the tutor opens the actual software — Final Cut Pro, or whatever the subject is — and performs the skill live in front of the learner, the way an instructor teaches in a college lab. Except this instructor is beside you, on your machine, in your environment, at your pace.

It **hands over**. The learner tries. The tutor watches.

It **observes and evaluates**. Real-time feedback as the learner works: mistakes spotted the moment they happen, capability honestly assessed from what the learner actually does, not what they claim.

It **adapts**. Pace, language — including mixed and local languages — depth, style, difficulty: continuously optimized to this one learner. The core of the product is one-to-one teaching, personalized in every dimension.

## The canonical scenario

Anup is a CSIT student who wants to learn video editing in Final Cut Pro. He downloads the app on his Mac and is greeted by the tutor. He asks it to teach him Final Cut Pro.

The tutor asks where to begin with theory and fundamentals, given the apps available on his machine — Figma, for example. Anup says Figma. The tutor opens Figma, creates a workspace — anups_finalcut_learning — and starts teaching the foundations of editing there, using the canvas the way a university teacher uses a blackboard: for explanation and for assessment.

When it's time for practical work, the tutor opens Final Cut Pro itself and teaches hands-on, the way teachers teach in college labs — demonstrating, then watching Anup try, correcting him in the moment, adjusting to how fast he learns and what he struggles with.

## The teaching loop

> **explain → demonstrate → learner attempts → observe → feedback → adapt → next concept**

This loop, running live on real software on the learner's own machine, *is* the product. Everything else is implementation.

## Concept principles

1. **Teach in the learner's real environment.** Their machine, their apps, their files. Skills learned where they'll be used transfer; skills watched in a video mostly don't.
2. **Demonstration is first-class.** The tutor shows, not just tells. Watching the skill performed live, narrated, in the real tool, is half of how humans learn from experts.
3. **Observation is what makes it a tutor.** A teacher who cannot see the student is a video course. Seeing the learner work — and responding to it — is the difference.
4. **Personal in every dimension.** Pace, language, prior knowledge, goals, confidence. No two learners get the same lesson.
5. **Assessment is continuous and honest.** Mastery is observed from real performance, not self-reported.
6. **Domain-general by design.** The concept is not a Final Cut Pro course. It is a universal tutor: anything teachable with these capabilities — software, tools, workflows, skills — is in scope. Software is the natural beginning, not the boundary.
7. **The learner is always in control.** Of their machine, their data, and the session. The tutor acts on the learner's computer only in service of the learner, with their knowledge and consent. Trust is a concept-level requirement, not a feature.

## What it is not

Not a task agent — it never simply does the work for the learner; it acts on the machine only to teach. Not a video course or any form of passive content. Not an in-app help widget owned by one software vendor — it is the *learner's* tutor, across everything they want to learn. Not a chatbot that can only talk about the skill without seeing or touching it.

## Why now

Computer-use agents have arrived, and every one of them is aimed at replacing human effort — while evidence mounts that delegating work to AI quietly erodes the very skills people need. The capability that causes the problem is the same capability that can solve it. A tutor built on computer use, pointed the opposite direction, arrives at exactly the moment the world starts asking for it.

## What this document deliberately does not decide

Platform and stack. The first domain and first audience. How demonstrations are rendered and made reliable. How observation is implemented. How control passes between tutor and learner. Curriculum format. Business model. Pricing. All of that is execution — important, contestable, and downstream of this concept.

If this document is handed to an engineering team or a coding agent: treat it as the product's constitution. Propose execution plans *against* it, make the trade-offs explicit, and change execution freely — but the loop, the principles, and the inversion are the product.
